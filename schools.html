In brief

  • Teachers: Community Park has had more students per teacher than the other three in nearly every year, and the fewest teachers since 2019–20.
  • Spending: above the other three's average only around 2022–23 and 2023–24, and the lowest of the four in 2024–25.
  • Staff by role: classroom teachers close to the others' but less steady; special education flat while the other schools added staff; the fewest art, music and PE teachers.
  • Growth and attendance: mixed test score growth; the lowest absenteeism of the four in the last two years.

Test score growth and attendance

Growth from the state tests in English language arts and math, taken in English in grades 3 to 5. Growth compares each student with students statewide who scored similarly the year before, so it depends less on who attends a school than raw scores do. There were no state tests in 2019–20 and 2020–21, and no growth scores in 2021–22. Growth still reflects how a school teaches as well as its resources, so it cannot show what caused a difference.

Left out: the state's published growth scores for 2022–23 and 2023–24 count students by the school zone they live in rather than the school they attend, as the superintendent told the Board on October 27, 2026 (video). In those two years the state lists more Community Park test-takers than the school had students in grades 3 to 5, which confirms it. In the other years the counts fit each school's enrollment.

What the Board's records say about the program
  • The program began in 2015 as a strand within Community Park and later became school-wide (superintendent, October 27, 2026). Kindergarten entry is by lottery and capped, and students cannot join after first grade.
  • On October 27, 2026 the superintendent reported that parents and staff saw Community Park as less supported than other schools, citing class sizes and an art position cut below full time (video). Public comments in 2025 had compared its kindergarten classes with smaller ones elsewhere (March 18, 2025).
  • Several program costs are recorded outside the school: the world languages and dual language supervisor is based at the high school, teacher coaching was paid from a federal grant, and busing students into and out of Community Park is a district transportation cost the superintendent called "very costly" (video). Others are coded to the school: a dual language supplies line, aide stipends and a summer program paid from federal Title I funds.
  • The April 2026 budget reduced Community Park's sections and aides to save money. In October 2026 the superintendent proposed turning it into a "Spanish-intensive" neighborhood school; the Board had not voted as of these records (April 28, 2026; October 27, 2026).

Sources: meeting videos linked above, and Board minutes on BoardDocs: kindergarten lottery protocol (February 20, 2024); coaching paid from federal Title II funds (August 29, 2017; September 24, 2019); dual language aide stipends and the Title I summer program (May 23, 2023); supervisor's position (personnel agenda, May 6, 2025); dual language supplies line (budget transfer report, September 30, 2025).

Sources: state per-pupil expenditures, 2023–24 and 2024–25. The remainder is the 2024–25 district total minus the schools and the central office, each weighted by its students.

What these figures cannot show
  • They cover the whole school. Every Community Park section is now in the program, so the program is inside these figures, but they do not separate its costs or say which teachers are bilingual.
  • Program costs such as a coordinator, curriculum, materials or training may be recorded at the district level and spread over all students, which would hide them from a school-by-school comparison.
  • The district decides how to split shared costs among its schools, the state does not audit that split, and the split changed sharply in 2024–25.
  • The district's own budget documents or a direct question to the district would be needed to see what the program itself costs.
